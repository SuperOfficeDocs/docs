Every company has its own needs for what to register about its customers, sales, projects, contracts, and so on. Administrators can add fields and tables in SuperOffice to customize the type of information that can be registered.

SuperOffice CRM and Service were once two separate applications, with different extensibility options. [**User-defined fields**][1] (udefs for short) belong in SuperOffice CRM, while [**extra tables**][3] (aka 3rd party tables) and [**extra fields**][2] belong in Service. The term **custom field** includes both user-defined fields and extra fields.

There are two ways to extend SuperOffice:

* **Add fields to entities.** These fields are called *custom fields*. For example, add a "Satisfied customer" field to contacts.
* **Add new entities.** These entities are called *custom objects*. For example, register the laptops you lease to customers as their own entity.

## Custom fields

There are two kinds of custom fields, depending on the entity:

| Kind | Added to | Managed in |
|---|---|---|
| [User-defined fields][1] (udefs) | Companies, contacts, sales, projects, follow-ups, and documents | **Settings and maintenance** > **Fields** |
| [Extra fields][2] | Requests, messages, other Service tables, and custom objects | **Settings and maintenance** > **System design** > **Tables** |

## Custom objects

A [custom object][3] is a new entity that you define yourself. For example, *Laptop*, *Lease*, or *Car*. You decide which fields the custom object has and how it relates to companies, contacts, requests, and other entities.

With CRM Suite **Growth** or higher, a custom object behaves like the standard entities in SuperOffice. Users can find it in the Navigator, add custom data from **New** in the top bar, and search for it. They can also create selections and dashboards, and see related custom data on a section tab, for example on the company.

In the database, a custom object is an extra table. Administrators create it in **Settings and maintenance** > **System design** > **Tables**.

## Examples

* **Satisfied customer (user-defined field).** Add a Yes/No field to contacts. Send a mailing where contacts state whether they are satisfied, and store the answer in the field.

* **Agreement expiry date (user-defined field).** Register the expiry date of each maintenance agreement on the company. Create a dynamic selection of the companies where the agreement expires within one month.

* **Template variables (custom fields).** Reuse the information from custom fields in documents and emails as template variables. Each field you add gets its own unique ID (Prog ID), which you use in documents to merge in contact-specific information. For example, an email about an expiring maintenance agreement can show the correct expiry date for each contact.

* **Laptop leases (custom objects).** Register each laptop you lease out, with model, serial number, purchase date, and price. Register each lease as another custom object related to the laptop, the company, the contact, and the sale. The Company screen then shows a **Leases** tab, and the laptop shows all its leases over time.

With a user-plan based license, extra tables require Development Tools. [Extra tables][3] are available in SuperOffice Service, and in SuperOffice CRM through web panels.

The CRM Suite plan sets the number of custom objects you can create. When you reach the limit, you cannot create more. See [plan limits][13].

## Layout of fields

User-defined fields are shown on the [More tab][12], sorted by their rank. With the [Screen Designer][14], you can place fields on any tab and design the layout of custom objects. A layout created in the Screen Designer overrides the position set on individual fields.
