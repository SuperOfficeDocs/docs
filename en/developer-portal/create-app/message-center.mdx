**Messages** is the single place in the Developer Portal where you handle support tickets from customers of your apps and follow up on requests related to publishing. You can read, filter, and reply to tickets, including with attachments, without leaving the portal. You decide whether to close each ticket yourself or hand it back to SuperOffice.

If you have a standard application, this is also where you answer customers whose support requests SuperOffice forwards to you. See [How support requests reach you](#how-support-requests-reach-you).

It collects messages from:

* **Request to publish** events for:
  * Application configuration
  * App Store information
  * Partner page information
* Problems reported by customers through the App Store **Report a problem** link. See [Report a problem tickets](#report-a-problem-tickets).
* Support tickets created from the **Failures** details page.
* Support requests that SuperOffice forwards to you. See [How support requests reach you](#how-support-requests-reach-you).
* Your emails sent to `appdev@superoffice.com` or `appstore@superoffice.com`.

  <Warning>

  Problems may occur if you use a different email address than the one you sign in to the Developer Portal with, or if the address is a duplicate in SuperOffice.

  </Warning>

* Notifications about Developer Portal changes.

<Note>

Messages is not for getting help with your own development. For that, contact [SuperOffice Support][1].

</Note>

## At a glance

Messages is located in the upper-right corner of the Developer Portal.

![Messages entry point in the Top bar -screenshot][img1]

Tickets are grouped into categories in the **Inbox** sidebar so you can see at a glance what needs attention. The count next to each category shows how many tickets it currently contains.

| Category | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **All** | Every ticket associated with you and your apps. |
| **Waiting for SuperOffice** | Tickets you have replied to and routed back to SuperOffice. |
| **Open** | Tickets where the next action is yours. |
| **Closed** | Tickets that have been resolved. |

![Messages inbox with sidebar categories -screenshot][img2]

<Tip>

The [Messages widget][3] on your Home dashboard mirrors these same categories, so you can spot tickets in the **Open** category at a glance without opening Messages.

</Tip>

## Filter and find tickets

The ticket list is a table you can filter one column at a time. Filters are applied on the server, so paging stays fast even with many tickets. Filter by:

* **Ticket Id**
* **From** - the sender of the ticket.
* **Title**
* **Partner** - useful if you belong to more than one partner.
* **App** - limit the list to tickets for one of your apps.
* **Last Changed** - pick a date range.

Combine the sidebar categories with the column filters to focus on, for example, every **Open** ticket for one app. The list is paginated - use the controls at the bottom to move between pages.

![Ticket list with per-column filters and a date range -screenshot][img3]

## Reply to a ticket

Open a ticket to see its full history. You can compose a reply in two places, and the difference is *who gets the reply by default*:

* **Reply All**, at the top of the ticket - pre-fills the editor with the ticket's full recipient list (everyone currently on the conversation).
* **Reply**, at the bottom of an individual message - pre-fills the editor with only the recipients of that specific message. Useful when you want to respond to one branch of a long conversation without including everyone.

In both cases, the recipients are only a starting point. You can adjust the **To**, **Cc**, and **Bcc** fields freely before sending, and the chip behavior is the same in both editors.

1. Click **Reply All** at the top of the ticket, or **Reply** at the bottom of an individual message.

1. (Optional) Expand **Cc** or **Bcc** to add more recipients, or click the X on a chip to drop someone from the reply.

1. Compose your message using the rich-text editor.

1. (Optional) [Add an attachment](#add-an-attachment).

1. Decide what happens to the ticket after you send - see [Mark as answered or Return to SuperOffice](#mark-as-answered-or-return-to-superoffice).

1. Click **Send**.

![Reply editor with To, Cc, and Bcc fields and the Mark as answered / Return to SuperOffice toggle -screenshot][img4]

### To, Cc, and Bcc addresses

The **To**, **Cc**, and **Bcc** fields accept multiple email addresses, shown as chips:

* Type an address and press **Enter**, or simply click outside the field, to confirm it. When you click **Send**, any address you typed but did not confirm is added automatically - addresses are no longer dropped silently.
* Invalid addresses appear as a red chip. **Send** is disabled until you remove or correct them.

### Add an attachment

You can attach **image files only**, up to **5 MB per file**. Add images one at a time - there is no overall cap on the number of attachments, only the per-file size limit.

To send an image with your reply:

1. Click the attachment icon in the reply editor.

1. Select an image from your computer.

1. The portal validates the file before adding it:

    * If it is not an image, the file is rejected with the message *Only image files are allowed as attachments.*
    * If it is larger than 5 MB, the file is rejected with the message *Attachment exceeds the maximum allowed size of 5 MB.*

1. Repeat to add more images if you need to.

1. Send the reply as usual.

![Attachment picker showing the image-only validation message -screenshot][img5]

### Mark as answered or Return to SuperOffice

Next to the **Send** button there is a toggle that decides what happens to the ticket *after* your reply goes out.

By default, the toggle is set to **Mark as answered**. Switch it to **Return to SuperOffice** to hand the ticket back. **Send** is then disabled until you add at least one recipient in **To**, **Cc**, or **Bcc**, so you can't hand a ticket back without telling the customer.

| Option | When to use it | What happens to the ticket |
|---|---|---|
| **Mark as answered** *(default)* | You have resolved the customer's question yourself. | The ticket is closed on the partner side and moves to the **Closed** category. You can still open it later from there. |
| **Return to SuperOffice** | You need SuperOffice to take it from here, or the request is outside the partner's scope. | Responsibility for the ticket moves back to SuperOffice. For **app support** tickets, the ticket also leaves your Messages entirely. It no longer belongs to the partner. The reply is still emailed to everyone listed in **To**, **Cc**, and **Bcc**. |

## How support requests reach you

If you have a **standard application** in the App Store, your customers are also SuperOffice customers. When they contact SuperOffice support about your app, SuperOffice forwards the request to you, and you answer the customer from **Messages**.

1. A SuperOffice support agent decides that the customer's request is about your app and forwards it to you.

1. Within about 30 minutes, you receive an email with a direct link to the ticket.

    ![Email notifying partner support about a new message to respond to -screenshot][img6]

1. Sign in to the Developer Portal and select the link, or go to **Messages**. The ticket is in the **Open** category.

1. Read the customer's request and [reply to the ticket](#reply-to-a-ticket). The customer receives your reply by email from the SuperOffice ticket, so you don't need to email them separately.

1. Choose **Mark as answered** if you resolved the request, or **Return to SuperOffice** if the request isn't for you or SuperOffice should take over. See [Mark as answered or Return to SuperOffice](#mark-as-answered-or-return-to-superoffice).

<Warning>

Always reply from Messages. The notification email only tells you that a customer is waiting for an answer. If you reply to the email, SuperOffice sees your message on the ticket, but the customer doesn't receive it.

</Warning>

If you haven't replied within 7 days, the ticket is reopened and you receive a new reminder email. The ticket stays in Messages, and you can still reply.

Problems that customers report through **Report a problem** in the App Store reach you the same way. See [Report a problem tickets](#report-a-problem-tickets).

<Note>

SuperOffice sends the notification to the **[Support emails][8]** of the partner that owns the app. If none are set, it uses the app's **[Error reporting emails][9]**, then the sign-in email of the owner partner's **technical contact**. This is the partner's technical contact, not the app's technical contact that [error emails][9] fall back to. If none of these are set, no one is notified, so make sure the owner partner has set **Support emails** in the partner settings.

</Note>

## Report a problem tickets

When a customer uses **Report a problem** on your app in the App Store:

* A ticket is created in your app support queue, and you're notified the same way as for [forwarded support requests](#how-support-requests-reach-you). You no longer receive the problem report itself by email.
* The details of the problem are on the ticket. Open it in Messages to read them and reply.
* The customer who reported the problem gets a confirmation by email.
* The ticket always shows the customer's tenant from the same environment as the App Store the problem was reported from.

Uninstall requests and requests to publish are handled as before.

## Open a ticket in the SuperOffice Customer Center

When you click a ticket ID, a new browser tab opens that ticket in the SuperOffice Customer Center.

<Danger>
You must be authenticated to see the ticket immediately in the SuperOffice Customer Center. Otherwise you will have to sign in.
</Danger>
